03 · OPERATE

Compliant dispatch, then autonomy you can argue with.

Every mission is checked against your assigned ruleset before it's created. A blocking rule refuses the mission — no row, no dispatch — and the refusal goes into the audit log. The scheduler and the integration hub run the same gate, so there is no automated-mission bypass lane. When the scheduler proposes an assignment it shows the score, the reasons, and why the other robot lost. It proposes; auto-apply is opt-in and off by default.

05 · GOVERN

Receipts, not assurances.

Fleet decisions are written to a per-org, hash-chained audit log that a verifier re-walks on a schedule and reports gaps in. Tenants are isolated at the query layer, credentials are encrypted and write-only, outbound calls are SSRF-guarded, and the compliance rulesets tell you what applies at your size. They're assistive checks, not certified safety systems — PRISM will never show you a green check it can't back up.

Ask Cortex

The advisor runs on your machine.

PRISM ships its own small advisor model — 753 KB, trained on the product's own vocabulary, pinned by checksum. In the public demo it runs entirely inside your browser over WebAssembly: no external language model, and nothing you type leaves the page. Press ⌘K in the demo and ask it something.

These are engineering figures from the codebase and its test suite — not production benchmarks, and not customer results. There are no customer results yet. The compliance rulesets are assistive checks, not certified safety systems.
  • A robot's state is on screen in about a second, and every reading is kept. (ingest → liveness → server-sent events → partitioned history)
  • A blocking compliance rule refuses a mission before it exists. No automated bypass lane.
  • A per-org hash-chained audit log, re-walked on a schedule by a verifier that reports gaps.
  • Health scores that raise an alert before a robot strands a job.
  • A scheduler that shows why this robot won and why the other one lost. It proposes; auto-apply is opt-in and off by default.
  • Multi-tenant isolation, SSO, MFA, encrypted write-only credentials, SSRF-guarded outbound calls, tiered entitlements.

Three vendor adapters behind one interface: ROS 2 / rosbridge, a generic-HTTP fallback for any REST vendor, and one named commercial fleet API.

A real-hardware bridge is built and containerized — consumer robots pushing into the exact production ingest path a commercial platform uses. The first timed onboarding run is the next step.

Where this actually is

Pre-revenue. No customers. Worth saying out loud.

PRISM is built and running, and it has no customers. No pilots, no logos, no case studies. If you came here looking for a "trusted by" band, there isn't one, and I'm not going to make one up.

It's built by one person plus a staff of AI agents, nights and weekends. Around 1,350 automated tests and a set of CI gates are how that stays honest rather than fast and wrong.

What's next isn't more code — it's the first site. If you run a multi-robot operation and this looks like your problem, that's the conversation I want to have.
